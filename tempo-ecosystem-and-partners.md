# Tempo Ecosystem, Partners, and Mainnet Status

## Overview

Tempo Mainnet launched on **March 18, 2026**. This document captures the key ecosystem context, partnerships, and product positioning as of launch.

---

## Origin and Backing

Tempo was **incubated by Paradigm and Stripe**.

This backing is significant because it connects Tempo directly to both a major crypto-native investment firm and one of the world's most important payment infrastructure companies. The Stripe connection is especially relevant to Tempo's product story, given that Stripe co-authored the Machine Payments Protocol (MPP) with Tempo.

---

## Mainnet Launch

Tempo Mainnet went live on March 18, 2026, with:

- public RPC endpoints
- the Machine Payments Protocol (MPP) as an open standard
- a payments directory with 100+ MPP-compatible services at launch
- institutional validators in a permissioned configuration (roadmap to permissionless)

Key infrastructure available at launch:

- Tempo Wallet (web and CLI)
- Block Explorer (explore.tempo.xyz)
- SDKs in TypeScript, Go, Python, and Rust
- Foundry tooling for smart contract development
- Fee sponsorship services
- Token list registry
- Contract verification services

## MPP Launch Partners

The Machine Payments Protocol launched with ecosystem integrations from:

- **Stripe** — Co-author of MPP; extended MPP to support cards, wallets, and other payment methods
- **Visa** — Extended MPP to support card-based payments on the Visa network
- **Lightspark** — Extended MPP for Bitcoin payments over the Lightning network

The MPP payments directory at launch included 100+ services spanning:

- Model providers
- Developer infrastructure
- Compute platforms
- Data services

Named launch integrations include **Alchemy**, **Dune Analytics**, **Merit Systems**, and **Parallel Web Systems**.

## Target Use Cases

Based on the mainnet launch materials, Tempo explicitly targets:

### Agentic Payments
- AI agents paying for services programmatically
- Pay-per-call APIs
- Monetized MCP servers
- Multi-service agent workflows
- Streaming/session-based machine payments

### Global Payouts
- Large-scale disbursements (payroll, seller payments, creator payments)
- Predictable costs via payment lanes
- Instant settlement

### Cross-Border Remittances
- International transfers that settle in seconds
- Transparent audit trail
- Predictable costs

### Embedded Finance
- Software companies embedding payment flows directly into products
- Smart accounts and protocol-level memos for ledger integration
- Developer tooling for building financial workflows

### Tokenized Deposits
- Financial institutions creating tokenized representations of deposits
- Continuous settlement (beyond banking hours)
- Reconciliation primitives and compliance registries

---

## Consensus and Validator Configuration

At mainnet launch:

- Simplex BFT consensus (via Commonware)
- Institutional validators in a permissioned configuration
- ~600ms block production under normal conditions
- Deterministic finality (no reorg risk after finalization)
- Roadmap to permissionless validation

---

## Network Parameters (TIP-1010)

- Base fee: 20 billion attodollars per gas (2 × 10^10)
- Total block gas limit: 500M gas
- General gas limit: 30M gas per block (~6% of total)
- Payment lane: ~94% of blockspace reserved for payment transactions
- Standard TIP-20 transfer cost: ~$0.001
